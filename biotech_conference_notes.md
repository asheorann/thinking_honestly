## Session 2: Commercial, Strategy & Marketing — The Art of Differentiation: Competing Beyond the Molecule

### Questions
- How do you differentiate beyond the drug/product itself as a biotech?
- What does it mean to incorporate the voice of the patient into clinical trial design?
- What does pharmacoeconomics mean?
- How much is patient trust vs. physician trust — which matters more?
- In the Bluebird Bio example: how do you bring the price point down? What are the elements that make a drug more or less expensive?
- How do you forecast something without previous data (new country, new product)? What is scenario forecasting?
- What is the general flow between quarters (commercial planning cycle)?

### Insights & Thoughts
- **Differentiation is in execution speed** — the molecules themselves are often not that different in efficacy or safety. Speed is the real differentiator.
- **First-in-class vs. best-in-class** is a key strategic framing for positioning a therapy.
- **The label is the most important thing** — it defines what you're legally allowed to promote.
- Veer case study vs. Gilead: Gilead had a massive head start in HIV medicine, but Veer built their entire identity around genuinely caring about patients — and that was their differentiator.
- Getting patient insight into clinical trial design is another differentiator (multiple panelists echoed this).
- Market access people who talk to physicians are critical — it's often a bidirectional exchange, and understanding doctors is as much a psychology challenge as a scientific one.
- Basis Health panelist made the point: the real reason companies fail isn't clinical trial speed — **it's the pricing**. Patients can't pay.
- The "no front door" problem: even though the healthcare system wants the drugs, they want them at a specific price point and for a specific indication. There's no unified entry point. Potential startup idea?
- Scarcity is a significant structural problem in this space — both technical solutions and governance solutions needed.
- The concept of "control towers" — different teams have their own towers, and those towers need to communicate with each other across the org.
- Listen to competitors. Large pharmas track and attend conferences *very* seriously.
- For a smaller biotech: get the word out early, social media matters.
- **Longevity** has many definitions right now — everything is claiming the label. One speaker's specific framing: either your body gives out or your mind gives out, so they're focusing squarely on neurodegenerative disorders.

### Insights & Thoughts
- BD is heavily a people-skills game. Being genuine matters.
- Always look through the lens of the other party when structuring a deal.
- EU regulatory: you can pursue either the full EU or a single country — generally single-country submission is the preferred starting path.
- A common pattern: tons of innovation and big ideas, but **follow-through all the way is what's actually hard**.
